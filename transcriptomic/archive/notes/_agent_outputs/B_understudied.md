# Schizophrenia Literature Review: Five SEA-AD Cortical Cell Types (Agent B — under-studied subtypes)

## Chandelier (axo-axonic PVALB interneuron)

**Top findings restated.** Chandelier neurons in SCZ show down-regulation of synaptic/postsynaptic membrane genes including KCNC1 (Kv3.1), VGF, FBLN7, and gated-channel pathways, alongside up-regulation of mTORC1 signaling, MYC targets, and LRRTM2-class synaptic adhesion.

**What's been reported before.** Chandelier cells are by far the best-studied of this group, almost entirely owing to the Lewis lab. The canonical finding is reduced density of GAT-1-immunoreactive axon "cartridges" innervating pyramidal-neuron axon initial segments in DLPFC layers 2-superficial 3, with reciprocal up-regulation of postsynaptic GABA-A α2 subunits on the AIS — first reported in [Woo et al. 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9560277/), elaborated in [Pierri et al. 1999](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/ajp.156.11.1709), [Volk et al. 2002](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/12/10/1063/268401), [Cruz et al. 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19322171/), and reviewed comprehensively in [Lewis 2011](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/dneu.20825) and [Rocco et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5374057/). Lower KCNS3 (Kv9.3) in PV neurons was reported by [Georgiev et al. 2014](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2013.13040468), and Kv3.1 (KCNC1) itself was found reduced in untreated SCZ DLPFC and normalized by antipsychotics ([Yanagi et al. / Dwivedi review, MDPI 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/1422-0067/25/16/8696)). VGF is a long-recognized SCZ-relevant neuropeptide precursor — reduced in DLPFC and hippocampus across major mental illnesses and downstream of DISC1 ([Thakker-Varia & Alder, 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4204764/); [Brain Communications review 2021](https://academic.oup.com/braincomms/article/3/4/fcab261/6412560)). mTORC1/AKT signaling dysregulation is established in SCZ DLPFC ([Chadha & Meador-Woodruff 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7162985/); [Ibarra-Lecue et al. 2020, S6 hypofunction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7105616/)), though direction varies by complex and region. LRRTM2 has not been directly implicated in SCZ, but its paralog LRRTM1 carries SCZ risk variants and regulates thalamic synaptic function ([Wen et al. 2021, Mol Psychiatry](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41380-021-01146-6)). FBLN7 has no direct SCZ literature — it is best known as an OPC-axon adhesion molecule ([Mol Med 2020](https://molmed.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s10020-020-00169-z)). For chandelier-specific snRNA-seq, recent work by the Lewis group ([Hoftman et al. AJP 2026](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.20250355)) explicitly resolves chandelier cells transcriptionally from PV basket cells.

**Verdicts.**
- Down-regulated synaptic/gated-channel pathway → **Known** (Lewis lab, decades).
- KCNC1 ↓ → **Known/Partial** (matches Kv3.1 literature; not previously localized to chandelier specifically).
- VGF ↓ → **Known** (cross-disorder, not chandelier-specific).
- mTORC1 ↑ → **Partial/Contradicts** — most evidence is for AKT/mTOR pathway *down*-regulation in bulk DLPFC; an up-regulation in chandelier specifically could reconcile by cell-type heterogeneity, but warrants follow-up.
- LRRTM2 ↑ → **Novel for chandelier**; family is SCZ-relevant.
- FBLN7 ↓, MICAL2, KLHL5, ADCY10 → **Novel.**

**Coverage caveats.** Most Lewis-lab cartridge work predates snRNA-seq, so gene-level comparisons are inferential. Some Biological Psychiatry / ScienceDirect Lewis papers are paywalled (abstracts only).

## Sst Chodl (long-range projecting NOS1+ GABA neuron)

**Top findings restated.** No genes pass padj<0.1, but pathway-level GSEA flags down-regulation of negative regulation of synaptic transmission and postsynaptic components, up-regulation of RNA localization and cytokine signaling.

**What's been reported before.** Direct SCZ literature on the Sst_Chodl subclass is *extremely sparse* in human postmortem work, because Sst_Chodl cells are ultra-rare (~0.001% of cortical neurons) and historically lumped under SST/NOS1+ interneurons. The most directly relevant recent paper is [Loh et al. 2025, Neuron](https://www.cell.com/neuron/abstract/S0896-6273(25)00663-4) (15q13.3 microdeletion mouse model): single-nucleus transcriptomics found the *largest* gene-expression alterations in Sst_Chodl, calcium imaging showed Sst_Chodl hyperactivity, and DEGs from this subtype were uniquely enriched for SCZ GWAS hits. The popular-press summary ([Brighter Side 2025](https://www.thebrighterside.news/post/brain-cell-discovery-offers-new-hope-in-preventing-schizophrenia/)) emphasizes the rarity-vs-importance contrast. Broader SST literature in SCZ is rich: SST mRNA reductions in OFC ([Joshi et al. 2015](https://www.nature.com/articles/npjschz20144)), DLPFC ([Fung et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8379548/)), and JAMA Psychiatry diagnostic specificity work on prefrontal SST neurons ([Volk et al. 2023](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/2808723)). The new [AJP 2026 paper](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.20250355) explicitly distinguishes Sst_Chodl as a discrete subtype in human DLPFC, setting the stage for direct SCZ tests.

**Verdicts.**
- Down-regulation of synaptic/postsynaptic transmission pathways → **Known** at the broader SST class level; **Novel** at the Sst_Chodl-specific level.
- Up-regulated cytokine signaling → **Partial** — inflammatory signatures are broadly reported in SCZ cortex ([Trépanier et al. 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/mp201690)) but not previously localized to this subtype.
- RNA localization up → **Novel.**
- All top gene-level hits (ENOX1, MAP3K13, SPATA6L, DNAH6) → **Novel** (no SCZ-specific literature found).

**Coverage caveats.** Honest summary: there is essentially **no direct human SCZ postmortem literature for Sst_Chodl specifically**. The 15q13.3 mouse work ([Loh 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40997796/)) is the closest analog and supports the *cell type* being uniquely vulnerable, even though direction-of-effect (hyperactivity in mouse vs. down-regulated inhibitory machinery in our data) requires careful interpretation.

## Pax6 (CGE-derived rare interneuron)

**Top findings restated.** 5/7161 genes pass padj<0.1, with down-regulated gated channel / cation-transport pathways prominent and a modest up-regulation of TF activity and male-gamete-generation pathways.

**What's been reported before.** No direct human postmortem SCZ literature on the *Pax6 interneuron subclass* was found. PAX6 itself is mostly studied as a developmental transcription factor coordinating cortical progenitor proliferation and cortical regional identity ([Manuel et al. 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26304102/); [Cocas et al. 2011, J Neurosci](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/31/14/5313)). The CGE more broadly contributes vulnerability — interneuron developmental disruption is a recurrent SCZ theme ([Wonders & Anderson, 2006-era reviews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganglionic_eminence); [PMC11349020 GABA/cholinergic prenatal](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11349020/)) and the Ruzicka, Batiuk and Hoftman atlases generally find CGE-derived populations under-explored relative to MGE-derived PV/SST. The down-regulated gated-channel signal echoes the broader cortical pattern reported by [Smolin et al. 2012, IJNP](https://academic.oup.com/ijnp/article/15/7/869/638379) on ion-channel subunit down-regulation in SCZ. Up-regulated DNA-binding TF activity is a recurrent but non-specific snRNA-seq pattern in SCZ cortex ([Batiuk et al. 2022](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.abn8367)).

**Verdicts.**
- Down-regulated gated channel / cation transport → **Known** (general SCZ pattern, **novel** for Pax6).
- TF activity up → **Partial.**
- Male-gamete-generation pathway up → **Novel** and likely a known artifact of dynein/ciliary/RNA-binding gene overlap (DNAH-family genes drive this signature in many tissues).
- Top genes (COL16A1, EPM2A, TXNDC15, PTBP1, ANKMY2, TSHZ1, CXXC4) → **Novel** to Pax6 in SCZ. PTBP1 is a canonical regulator of neuronal splicing and miRNA-124 biogenesis ([Boutz et al. 2007 / PMC3422667](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3422667/)) but has no direct SCZ-postmortem report.

**Coverage caveats.** Pax6 interneurons are essentially unstudied in human SCZ postmortem at gene-level resolution. Statements above are framed accordingly.

## L5 ET (extratelencephalic / pyramidal-tract)

**Top findings restated.** 3/4162 genes pass padj<0.1 — top down hits PDE4B, MFN1 are mechanistically suggestive; top up hit ALDH3A2 is striking; pathway-level signal is weak (lowest padj ~0.19 for presynapse).

**What's been reported before.** L5 ET neurons are scarce in human cortex compared with rodent ([Berg et al. 2021, Neuron](https://www.cell.com/neuron/fulltext/S0896-6273(21)00627-9?uuid=uuid:38e27516-023c-4551-924c-44710dd89772)), and snRNA-seq sample sizes are correspondingly small. Most prior layer-5 SCZ work focuses on the broader L5_6_FEZF2 family, where [Batiuk et al. 2022](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.abn8367) reported *increased* proportional abundance in DLPFC SCZ relative to controls, with most subtypes affected. PDE4B is one of the most-replicated SCZ genes: it interacts with DISC1 to regulate cAMP signaling ([Millar et al. 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16293762/)) and is down-regulated in SCZ DLPFC across multiple cohorts ([Fatemi et al. 2008](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0920996408000923); [meta-analysis in Genes 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4425/15/5/609)). MFN1 is in the mitochondrial-fusion machinery; mitochondrial-pathway dysregulation is a robust SCZ DLPFC signature in bulk and in layer 3/5 pyramidal-neuron LCM transcriptomics ([Roberts 2021 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7810242/); [Hjelm et al. 2015 / PMC8195258](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8195258/)). Dendritic-spine deficits in L5 pyramidal neurons are well-described ([Glausier & Lewis 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22546337/)). ALDH3A2 (fatty-aldehyde dehydrogenase; Sjögren-Larsson gene) has no direct SCZ literature.

**Verdicts.**
- PDE4B ↓ → **Known** (replicates a strong literature, here localized to L5 ET).
- MFN1 ↓ → **Partial/Known** — fits the mitochondrial-dysfunction signature in L5 pyramidal neurons.
- Down-regulated presynapse / adaptive-immune pathways → **Partial** (presynaptic deficits known; adaptive-immune signal unusual).
- ALDH3A2 ↑ → **Novel.**
- Other top genes (SPRY4-AS1, COL12A1, ANKRD28, ANKDD1A, RIPOR2, PANTR1) → **Novel.**

**Coverage caveats.** Low statistical power (only 3 padj<0.1 hits across 4,162 tested genes) means most signals are tentative; rarity of L5 ET in human cortex is the dominant constraint.

## L5_6 NP (near-projecting)

**Top findings restated.** 15/9789 genes pass padj<0.1; the standout pathway signal is strong down-regulation of cholesterol/sterol biosynthesis (REACTOME and GOBP, padj 0.005-0.04). Top gene hits include BICDL1, GPC1, CDK9, TRPM2 (down) and RAB3IP, PAPOLA (up).

**What's been reported before.** Direct SCZ literature on the L5/6 NP subclass is essentially absent. NP neurons are anatomically defined by intracortical projections and are taxonomically distinct from IT/ET/CT (Allen / SEA-AD taxonomy; [CellGuide entry](https://cellxgene.cziscience.com/cellguide/CL:4023043)). The cholesterol-biosynthesis down-regulation is striking and aligns with a recognized SCZ signature: cholesterol metabolism is implicated in SCZ pathophysiology ([Petrov et al. 2017, PMC10175956](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10175956/); [neuronal cholesterol synthesis review, PMC7184320](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7184320/)), and most antipsychotics (haloperidol, clozapine, aripiprazole) modulate SREBP-driven lipogenic transcription including HMGCR/DHCR7 ([Fernø et al. 2005, PMC1635424](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1635424/)). A confound: medicated SCZ patients can have *up-regulated* cholesterol-synthesis genes from antipsychotic exposure, so the direction we observe is interesting and should be cross-checked against medication status. GPC1 (glypican-1) is a heparan-sulfate proteoglycan with GWAS-implicated SCZ association and altered GPI-anchored protein processing in SCZ frontal cortex ([Williams et al. 2018, PMC6341114](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6341114/)). Synaptic-vesicle / RAB-family trafficking is a longstanding SCZ presynaptic theme ([Egbujo et al. 2016, PMC5134737](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5134737/)). CDK9/P-TEFb has neuronal-immediate-early-gene roles but no SCZ-specific literature.

**Verdicts.**
- Cholesterol biosynthesis ↓ → **Known at brain level, novel at L5_6 NP** — and directionally interesting given antipsychotic effects.
- GPC1 ↓ → **Partial/Known.**
- RAB3IP ↑ → **Partial** (RAB3-family vesicle trafficking is a SCZ theme).
- BICDL1, TRPM2, CDK9, PAPOLA, VWA3A, HOOK1 → **Novel.**

**Coverage caveats.** No NP-specific SCZ literature exists. The cholesterol signal is the most publishable finding; cross-check against medication and CSF lipid data is advisable.
