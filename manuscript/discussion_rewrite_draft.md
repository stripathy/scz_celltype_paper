Whether cortical inhibitory interneurons in schizophrenia (SCZ) are lost or persist in an altered molecular state has been contested for two decades. Across a seven-dataset snRNAseq meta-analysis, Xenium spatial replication, common-variant genetics anchored to patch-seq physiology, and a cross-disorder comparison against the Alzheimer's disease (AD) pseudo-progression trajectory, both accounts hold, in partly different cells: a subclass-wide, cell-intrinsic reduction of SST mRNA within Sst neurons co-occurs with depletion of specific upper-layer Sst supertypes, set against an increase in L6b excitatory neurons. The depleted population is CALB1-expressing, enriched for SCZ common-variant risk, and declines in parallel along the AD trajectory — properties that mark an intrinsically vulnerable cell population rather than the target of a disease-specific lesion.

The two accounts arose from largely separate literatures: an altered-state view built on in situ and microarray evidence of lowered SST and GABA-synthetic transcripts without detectable cell loss (Volk et al., 2000; Hashimoto et al., 2003; Dienel et al., 2022, 2023), and a cell-loss view supported by histological and transcriptomic counting studies (Benes et al., 1991; Beasley et al., 2002; Toker et al., 2018; Batiuk et al., 2022; Kiss et al., 2026). Granular taxonomy dissolves the conflict: at subclass resolution a pan-Sst molecular down-shift and a subtype-restricted loss of cells are confounded, whereas at supertype resolution they separate cleanly — a ~26% reduction (95% CI 14–36%) of a single supertype (Sst_25) is nearly invisible averaged over the whole subclass, explaining how coarser studies could disagree.

The depletion is tiered and laminar. Sst_2, Sst_3 and Sst_25 are FDR-significant in the meta-analysis, replicate in Xenium, and survive re-annotation excluding SCZ-DE genes; Sst_22 and Sst_20 (the latter nominal-only in the meta-analysis) did not replicate in Xenium, consistent with their limited a priori resolvability on the 300-gene panel (Supplementary Fig. S2; Supplementary Methods SM1), and Sst_22 also lost significance under re-annotation, so we treat both as provisional. Across all 16 Sst supertypes, more superficial types were more depleted (ρ = 0.76; Fig. 3f), and an orthogonal, histology-based RNAscope re-analysis in the subgenual anterior cingulate (Arbabi et al., 2025) found a trend-level SST density reduction, larger in upper than deeper layers (Supplementary Fig. S14), directionally consistent with the frontal-cortex laminar gradient in a second cortical region.

Cross-platform behavior of the two canonical markers is itself informative. The SST reduction held in Xenium with the same direction and similar magnitude (P = 0.052), and concordance extended panel-wide (Pearson r = 0.73; 76% sign agreement; Fig. 2j). Because SST transcription is activity- and BDNF-dependent [TODO: cite BDNF–SST cascade, e.g., Tripp et al.; Guilloux et al.], the pan-Sst reduction, though measured within Sst cells, may index reduced excitatory drive onto them rather than a cell-autonomous defect — consistent with concurrent BDNF down-regulation in L2/3 IT neurons (Fig. 2j). PVALB, by contrast, was unchanged in snRNAseq but nominally reduced in Xenium (P = 0.044), consistent with a partly cytoplasmic PVALB transcript captured by whole-cell Xenium and under-sampled by nucleus-restricted snRNAseq [TODO: cite PVALB mRNA subcellular localization]; earlier tissue-based reports of lower PV mRNA (Hashimoto et al., 2003) likewise sampled cytoplasm, reconciling the historical PV findings with our snRNAseq null. We read SST as the robust, cross-platform expression finding and PVALB as platform-dependent.

A deeper ambiguity remains. Because SCZ alters the transcripts used to assign cell identity, an apparently depleted supertype may reflect bona fide neuronal loss or erosion of transcriptomic identity below the threshold of confident classification — the whole-transcriptome form of the detection confound that has long complicated marker-based counting (Dienel et al., 2023). Cross-platform replication and the DE-gene-excluded re-annotation narrow that possibility without eliminating it: the re-annotation removes detected DE genes only, and supertype-level DE was underpowered, so sub-threshold expression shifts could still erode classifiability. For the expression finding the same circularity is conservative — cells whose SST expression collapses are less likely to retain the Sst label, biasing the within-Sst estimate toward the null. We therefore frame the abundance results as identifying which cells are most affected, and where, remaining agnostic between loss and identity erosion.

More than twenty years ago, reduced density of calbindin-immunoreactive interneurons was reported in SCZ prefrontal cortex (Beasley et al., 2002); our data localize that observation to named transcriptomic supertypes. The depleted supertypes carry a coherent molecular signature (234 genes; Fig. 4h) including elevated CALB1 (Fig. 4i), and in primates CALB1-expressing interneurons include the double-bouquet cell — a dendrite-targeting morphology with no clear rodent counterpart (Ballesteros-Yáñez et al., 2005; Raghanti et al., 2010) — to which the depleted supertypes partly correspond (Fig. 4f). Histological reports of calbindin density are mixed, with increases also described [TODO: cite Daviss & Lewis, 1995]; disaggregating the several CALB1-expressing populations may explain the discrepancy. The absence of the double-bouquet specialization in rodents leaves standard mouse models blind to this axis of pathology; mechanistic follow-up will require human or primate systems.

Common-variant genetics places the depleted population closer to cause than consequence. Germline risk is fixed before onset, so cell types enriched for that risk are unlikely to have acquired their association through chronicity or medication; across the 16 Sst supertypes, genetic enrichment tracked depletion (ρ = 0.56; Fig. 4a) [NOTE: PGC3-rendered value — co-update with Fig. 4 if the GWAS switches to Bigdeli]. The alignment does not establish that the abundance change is itself causal, and the convergence is sensitive to GWAS version: robust under PGC3 (Trubetskoy et al., 2022), the genetics–depletion correlation and the depth gradient of genetic enrichment (distinct from the compositional depth gradient of Fig. 3f) attenuate to non-significance under the larger Bigdeli et al. (2026) statistics (Supplementary Fig. S13; Supplementary Methods SM3) [TODO: state which genetics results are version-robust per S13/SM3]. Gene-level decomposition nominated HCN1 — fine-mapped as the likely causal gene at its locus (Fig. 4c), specifically expressed in Sst_25, its expression tracking both depletion (ρ = 0.79) and HCN-dependent voltage sag (ρ = 0.65) across supertypes. HCN1 loss-of-function causes an epileptic channelopathy [TODO: cite Nava et al., 2014], and I_h shapes dendritic integration [TODO: cite Magee] — converging with double-bouquet inhibition on the dendritic compartments of upper-layer pyramidal cells. Whether HCN1/I_h physiology is mechanistically tied to the cells' vulnerability, or simply marks their shared upper-layer identity, remains to be tested.

Our second compositional finding — increased abundance of two L6b excitatory supertypes (L6b_1, L6b_4), replicated in Xenium — parallels the long-standing excess of interstitial white-matter neurons in SCZ (Akbarian et al., 1993, 1996) without repeating that measurement: our spatial analysis excluded white matter, so the excess lies within gray-matter L6b, consistent with a subplate-lineage abnormality expressed on both sides of the L6b/white-matter boundary [TODO: cite subplate development, e.g., Kanold & Luhmann, 2010]. Label drift from adjacent deep-layer types is unlikely: both supertypes survive DE-gene-excluded re-annotation (L6b_1 β = 0.46, FDR = 0.0019; L6b_4 β = 0.32, FDR = 0.0019) and replicate spatially. Together with germline risk concentrated in the depleted Sst supertypes, a retained excess of subplate derivatives points to early-established rather than degenerative processes, though whether the two compositional changes share a developmental origin remains open.

The depleted Sst supertypes are not vulnerable only in schizophrenia. Sst supertypes are among the earliest populations lost as AD advances — an observation reported with the SEA-AD resource itself (Gabitto et al., 2024) — so the AD-side vulnerability is established; what has been untested is whether the cells affected in SCZ are the same ones. Because SEA-AD supplies the taxonomy used throughout our study, we could make that comparison exactly, supertype to supertype, relating each Sst supertype's SCZ compositional change to its decline along the AD continuous pseudo-progression score in DLPFC: the two profiles agreed in direction for 94% of supertypes (Spearman ρ = 0.87; n = 16 supertypes; Fig. 4j). The comparison crosses designs and demographics — a case-control diagnostic contrast versus a neuropathological progression score in aged donors — so the concordance identifies which cells are vulnerable, not a shared mechanism, and the time-courses almost certainly differ: progression-linked decline in AD versus a likely earlier, more static profile in SCZ. The shared taxonomy also cuts both ways: supertypes with weakly separable identities could appear depleted under any transcriptional perturbation in both datasets, though the DE-gene-excluded re-annotation argues against classification fragility on the SCZ side [TODO: state whether an equivalent AD-side robustness check exists]. Cortical somatostatin deficits were among the first neurochemical findings in AD (Davies et al., 1980); the present correspondence sharpens both literatures to the same named supertypes. Depletion of one population by a germline-anchored, presumably early process in SCZ and by a late, pathology-graded process in AD is what a vulnerability intrinsic to the cells, rather than to either disease, predicts (Saxena and Caroni, 2011). Whether the same population also declines in normal aging is plausible given documented age-related cortical SST decline (Erraji-Benchekroun et al., 2005), but untested here.

Limitations. Beyond the loss-versus-identity-erosion ambiguity above, meta-analysis privileges effects reproducible across datasets over large but dataset-specific ones. Cross-sectional post-mortem sampling cannot order molecular and compositional change; covarying age, sex and post-mortem interval addresses demographics and tissue quality but cannot address exposures collinear with diagnosis, notably antipsychotic medication and illness chronicity [TODO: verify whether a donor age restriction was applied; "under 70" is not documented in the Results text]. Germline risk fixed before treatment argues against a purely iatrogenic account, as would the parallel decline along the AD trajectory if SEA-AD donors were antipsychotic-naive [TODO: verify SEA-AD donor medication status] [TODO: cite antipsychotic-exposed non-human-primate studies]. Supertype resolvability on the 300-gene Xenium panel is limited, so subclass-level spatial conclusions are firmer than individual supertype allocations; supertype-level case-control DE was likewise underpowered, which is why the depleted-versus-not-depleted signature derives from the SEA-AD neurotypical reference rather than from SCZ cases. Finally, our datasets are predominantly DLPFC (one dataset, Fröhlich, is OFC — where Sst_25 independently reached nominal significance); the sgACC re-analysis provides preliminary cross-region support, but generalization requires additional cortical areas.

Pairing fine-grained taxonomies with cross-dataset meta-analysis offers a generalizable route to cell-type-resolved neuropathology in psychiatric disorders, where individual post-mortem datasets are small and heterogeneous. The present results set concrete next steps: expression-independent labels (protein, morphology, or lineage) to settle loss versus identity erosion; aging and longitudinal designs to test how far the vulnerability generalizes; and pharmacology aimed at the circuit these cells define — α5-subunit-containing GABA-A receptors, the principal mediators of dendrite-targeting Sst inhibition [TODO: cite α5-GABA-A PAM preclinical work, e.g., Prevot et al.], and HCN1/I_h itself — as entry points toward the alternative drug targets that treatment-resistant SCZ demands. The cells at the center of these priorities — upper-layer, CALB1-expressing, HCN1-high Sst interneurons, depleted in schizophrenia and declining along the AD trajectory — constitute an intrinsically vulnerable population, and now a named, testable one.
