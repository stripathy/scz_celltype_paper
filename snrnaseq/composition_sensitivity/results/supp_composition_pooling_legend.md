# Legend draft for results/supp_composition_pooling.{png,pdf}

**Supplementary Fig. SX | Robustness of the snRNA-seq cell-type abundance results to individual datasets and to the pooling strategy.**
(**a**) SCZ abundance change (crumblr β; orange, higher relative abundance in SCZ; blue, lower; clipped at ±0.5) for the 109 neuronal supertypes, in the order of Fig. 3a and grouped by subclass (colored strip). Top block, each dataset analyzed on its own: the seven snRNA-seq datasets and the Xenium dataset (as in Fig. 3e), with control and SCZ donor numbers in parentheses; dot, nominal P < 0.05. Lower block, estimates pooled across the seven snRNA-seq datasets: a random-effects meta-analysis of the per-dataset estimates; two mega-analyses that fit all 469 donors in a single model with dataset as a random intercept, without or with a dataset-specific random SCZ slope; and, set apart at the bottom, the fixed-effect meta-analysis used in the paper (Methods). Marks give the FDR within each pooled analysis (\*\*\* < 0.01, \*\* < 0.05, \* < 0.10, + < 0.20); the number of supertypes at FDR < 0.20 is given at right. The gray row gives between-dataset heterogeneity (I²) from the random-effects meta-analysis. Gray cells, supertype not resolved in that dataset or model not converged. Supertype names are styled as in Fig. 3a (red bold, FDR < 0.10; red italic, FDR < 0.20 in the paper's analysis). (**b**) β under each alternative pooling strategy against β from the paper's fixed-effect meta-analysis, for all 109 supertypes; dashed line, identity; ρ, Spearman; red and labeled, the 11 supertypes at FDR < 0.20 in the paper. (**c**) Leave-one-dataset-out fixed-effect meta-analysis for those 11 supertypes; diamond and bar, estimate and 95 % CI from all seven datasets; circles, the seven estimates obtained with one dataset left out.

## Key numbers (results/composition_pooling_sensitivity.csv; threshold FDR < 0.20)
- Donor counts in the row labels: from `data/neuron_counts_469donors.csv` (MSSM 2 140/45, HBCC 79/51, Fröhlich 29/32, MSSM 1 17/18, McLean 18/14, Batiuk 10/5, Multiome 5/6 control/SCZ; 298/171 in total) and `spatial/output/crumblr/crumblr_input_supertype_neuronal.csv` (Xenium 12/12).
- Per-dataset rows (results/composition_per_dataset_estimates.csv; Xenium from spatial/output/crumblr/crumblr_results_supertype_neuronal.csv): nominal P < 0.05 in 2–12 supertypes per snRNA-seq dataset (MSSM 1 12, Fröhlich 10, Batiuk 7, McLean 7, HBCC 4, MSSM 2 4, Multiome 2) and 25 in Xenium. For the 11 paper hits the single-dataset sign agrees with the pooled sign in 5–7 of 7 datasets (all 7 for Sst_2/3/22/25 and L6b_1), but each is individually significant in only 1–2 datasets (L6b_1 in 4). Xenium (Pvalb_14 not resolved; 106 shared supertypes, Spearman ρ = 0.40 vs FE β): same sign for 8 of the 10, nominal P < 0.05 for Sst_25 (−0.66), Sst_3 (−0.56), Sst_2 (−0.31), L6b_1 (+0.80), L6b_4 (+1.05), L6 CT_1 (+0.78).
- Rebuilt FE meta-analysis reproduces the published results exactly (Spearman ρ = 1.00 across 109 supertypes; identical β/P).
- Agreement of β with FE: RE ρ = 0.98; mega random-intercept ρ = 0.93; mega random-slope ρ = 0.89.
- A mega model with dataset as a fixed covariate (instead of a random intercept) is fit by `01` and kept in the CSV ("mega: dataset fixed") but not shown: it is numerically the same as the random-intercept model (β Pearson r = 0.9996, max |Δβ| = 0.010, SEs within 1 %, identical FDR < 0.01/0.05/0.10/0.20 sets). The random-intercept version is shown because the next row (adding a random SCZ slope) then differs from it by one term only.
- At FDR < 0.20: FE 11 (Sst_2/3/20/22/25, L6b_1/4, Pvalb_14, L2/3 IT_7, L6 CT_1, L5/6 NP_4); RE 5 (Sst_2, Sst_22, Sst_25, L6b_1, L6b_4); mega random-intercept 21 (all 11 except L5/6 NP_4 and L6 CT_1, plus ten more); mega random-slope 6 (Sst_2, Sst_3, Sst_22, L6b_1, L6b_4, L5/6 NP_2); three supertypes (Sst_20, Pvalb_8, Sst Chodl_1) did not converge under the random-slope model.
- Signs of all 11 are preserved under every strategy and in every leave-one-out fit. I²: Sst_2 1 %, Sst_3 26 %, Sst_20 40 %, Sst_22 18 %, Sst_25 2 %, L6b_4 10 %, L6b_1 65 % (RE β = 0.63 vs FE 0.48).
- Leave-one-out (results/composition_lodo.csv): every estimate keeps its sign, and 83 of the 84 leave-one-out estimates lie within the 95 % CI of the full estimate (the exception is L6 CT_1 without MSSM 2, 0.46 vs upper limit 0.41). Omitting Fröhlich attenuates the Sst supertypes most (β change Sst_2 −12 %, Sst_25 −18 %, Sst_22 −23 %, Sst_3 −39 %, Sst_20 −46 %; nominal P without Fröhlich 6 × 10⁻⁴, 0.024, 0.018, 0.10, 0.24 respectively). With FDR < 0.20 recomputed within each re-analysis: Sst_2 and L6b_1 hold in all seven; Sst_3, Sst_22 and Sst_25 drop below FDR 0.20 only without Fröhlich (because fewer hits make the BH correction harsher, not because the estimates change sign); L6b_4 only without MSSM 2; Sst_20 and the trend-level excitatory/Pvalb supertypes in several of the seven.
- Composite Sst (results/composition_subclass_lodo.csv, `code/01b_fit_subclass_models.R`; 18 neuronal subclasses): β = −0.17 (95 % CI −0.26 to −0.07), P = 4.8 × 10⁻⁴, FDR 0.009, I² = 0, negative in all seven datasets (Fröhlich P = 0.005 is the only individually significant one); leave-one-out β −0.13 to −0.21, all P ≤ 0.015 (the weakest without Fröhlich).

## Provenance
- `code/01_fit_pooling_models.R` (crumblr 1.0.0 + variancePartition::dream 1.38.1, lmer backend; metafor FE/REML) on `data/neuron_counts_469donors.csv` (from Nicole's `7_cohorts_metadata_names.csv`, 2026-08-19; Sex/Diagnosis harmonized; dataset names MSSM→MSSM 2, MtSinai→MSSM 1, OFC→Fröhlich; NaN counts = 0).
- Per-dataset models ~ dx + age_z + sex + PMI_z (PMI omitted for Multiome; age/PMI standardized within dataset; supertypes absent from a dataset dropped). Mega models: age/PMI standardized across donors; missing PMI (Multiome; one zero-coded MSSM 2 donor) set to the mean. PMI is in hours in all datasets.
- `code/01b_fit_subclass_models.R` → `results/composition_subclass_estimates.csv`, `results/composition_subclass_lodo.csv` (per-dataset crumblr on subclass-aggregated counts, FE meta, leave-one-out).
- `code/02_plot_pooling_heatmap.R` (7.1 × 8.6 in, cowplot 7 pt). Xenium row read from `spatial/output/crumblr/crumblr_results_supertype_neuronal.csv` (the supertype crumblr run quoted in the Results; Lamp5_Lhx6_1, Pvalb_3, Pvalb_14 absent). That run uses all 24 Xenium donors, no donor exclusions: QC-pass cortical cells only, supertypes present in at least half the donors (108 → 106), model ~ diagnosis + sex + age + PMI.
