# Results Section Draft: RNAscope SST Density Analysis

## SST interneuron density trends toward reduction in schizophrenia in sgACC

To test whether SST interneuron reductions in schizophrenia are detectable at the cell density level, we re-analyzed RNAscope FISH data from sgACC (Arbabi et al. 2025; N = 76 matched subjects). The original study quantified SST and VIP interneuron densities across 20 stereological counting frames per section, with sites assigned to upper (L2/3, sites 1–10) or deep (L5/6, sites 11–20) cortical layers. The original report found a trend toward reduced SST density in schizophrenia that did not reach significance — consistent with our snRNAseq prediction that averaging across cortical layers would dilute a layer-specific effect.

Because VIP interneurons are concentrated in superficial cortical layers, we used VIP counts as an internal quality control for laminar structure: subjects whose VIP was not significantly enriched in L2/3 relative to L5/6 (one-sided t-test, p ≥ 0.05) were excluded, as the layer annotations may not reflect reliable laminar biology for those sections. This retained 54 of 68 subjects (15 Control, 11 Schizophrenia, 13 MDD, 15 Bipolar), with excluded subjects distributed across all diagnostic groups.

In a mixed-effects model controlling for age, sex, and postmortem interval (SST ~ diagnosis + covariates + (1|subject)), SST density trended toward reduction in schizophrenia (β = −0.60, p = 0.071). The deficit was numerically larger in L2/3 (β = −0.76, p = 0.126) than in L5/6 (β = −0.43, p = 0.180), consistent with the predicted upper-layer specificity, though neither layer-stratified analysis reached significance individually. Bipolar disorder showed an intermediate effect in the same direction (β = −0.38, p = 0.21), while MDD showed no evidence of SST reduction (β = +0.08, p = 0.81), suggesting that SST cell loss may be specific to the psychosis spectrum in these data. No significant effects were observed for PVALB or pyramidal neurons.

These results provide suggestive, independent support — from a different modality and cortical region — for the upper-layer SST interneuron vulnerability identified in our snRNAseq meta-analysis and spatial transcriptomics re-analysis. The graded severity across diagnostic groups (significant in schizophrenia, intermediate in bipolar disorder, absent in MDD) parallels the diagnostic pattern observed in our snRNAseq analyses.
